# Amaryllis — Hippeastrum puniceum

Research reviewed 12 October 2026. This dossier guides a dedicated authored 3D
study; inventory mapping and visual plausibility do not establish scientific validation.

## Source-backed traits

Hippeastrum has a stout leafless scape, two enclosing spathe bracts, trumpets of six basally fused tepals, six stamens, a trifid stigma and smooth strap foliage.

[Primary reference](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/hippeastrum/)
[Additional reference](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:122815-2).

## Authored implementation

Two orange-red flowers and one younger bud, a short continuous basal tube, unequal broad tepals, arched filaments and a three-branched stigma.

Foliage: Long, thick strap blades in a basal fan.

Behavior: Heavy funnels nod slowly; tepal rims yield independently and recover elastically. Bloom can be reversed for inspection; this is not a
claim of biological reopening. Contact, stiffness, damping, colors, counts of
flowers/leaves, dimensions and stage timing are authored. Soft tissue uses sealed
parametric shells with separate folded positions/normals; fine reproductive organs
retain real geometry. Shared atlas sampling supplies cell/pigment irregularity,
with restrained scatter and distinct tissue roughness. Focused/selected physics
uses the existing 2,048/512-node budget; distant views remain ambient.

## Evidence gaps

Common-name Amaryllis here denotes Hippeastrum, not Amaryllis belladonna. Kew accepts H. puniceum; genus-level organ traits inform an authored species study, with no cultivar identity claimed. No measured elastic constants, spectral pigment data or tissue scans are
available. Full seasonal growth, pollinator action and fertilization are outside
this study. Review front, side, 45 degrees, macro, closed bud and half bloom before
calling the specimen finished.
