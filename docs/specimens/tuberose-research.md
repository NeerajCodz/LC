# Tuberose — Agave amica / Polianthes tuberosa

Sources reviewed October 13, 2026. The catalog concept is _Agave amica_
(Medik.) Thiede & Govaerts, matching the pinned WFO inventory candidate
`wfo-0001425429`. [NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/agave-amica/)
uses that name and lists _Polianthes tuberosa_ L. as its former name.
[Current Kew POWO](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:318009-2/general-information)
instead accepts _Polianthes tuberosa_ and includes _Agave amica_ among synonyms.
These are differing source treatments of the tuberose concept, not a universal
direction of synonymy. Preserve both names and source dates without changing
the pinned inventory. Depict a cultivated single six-lobed white form rather
than asserting a reconstructed extant wild population or named cultivar.

[Flora of Pakistan](https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=5&taxon_id=200028073)
describes paired waxy white flowers, a tubular or funnel-shaped perianth with
short curved subequal lobes, six stamens attached high inside its tube and
three stigmas.
[Smithsonian's Ornamental Garden Plants of the Guianas](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/sites/default/files/media/file/4ornamental-garden-plants-guianaspart-3.pdf)
documents six tepals united below into a long tube bent near its base, paired
bracteate flowers, spreading lobes, and an inferior three-celled ovary. It also
notes a cultivated double variant with twelve segments; that form is not this
six-lobed study. NC State documents bottom-to-top opening and smooth, slightly
succulent, entire strap leaves in a basal rosette.
[Missouri Botanical Garden](https://www.missouribotanicalgarden.org/PlantFinder/PlantFinderDetails.aspx?taxonid=275813)
confirms elongated flowering spikes above grasslike basal foliage and a long
cultivation history; its account treats the plant as a cultigen.

Authored composition: six flower pairs and one younger terminal pair
on one scape. Each flower has a continuous sealed hollow tube bent near its
base, six integrated fleshy flaring lobes, six included filament/anther units,
and a style with three stigma divisions. Keep the inferior ovary below the
perianth insertion; do not borrow Hyacinth's exposed superior ovary. Paired
flowers retain their shared bract/node arrangement. Five basal strap leaves
and reduced upper scape leaves/bracts are authored sampling choices. A curved
tube can introduce slight bilateral form despite its six-lobed mouth.

Opening upward along the spike is sourced; exact delays, pair spacing, bud
curvature, unfolding, ivory pigment, wall thickness, organ dimensions and
elastic response are authored. Folded organs follow the enclosing tube and
retain corresponding folded normals. Never substitute six detached radial
petals or mix twelve-segment double-flower anatomy into this single form.
Reversal is an inspection convention, not biological reopening. Scent
emission, pollination, ovary development and calibrated optical or mechanical
measurements are outside this study.

The closed distal wall keeps a small positive inner radius at its full authored
thickness. Its former narrow tip made the inner skin cross the axis and turn
over; tiny six-fold corrugations also intersected in persistent young buds.
A smooth distal-only radius margin and slower lobe emergence preserve a
continuous wall while leaving the fully open adult form and basal tube intact.
The margin and cubic lobe-emergence curve are authored mechanical/shape choices,
not measured bud anatomy. Actual generated inner-ring radius, opposing terminal
skin orientation and nonadjacent triangle intersections are checked for adult
and persistent-young tissue in all five qualities and seven morph fractions.
The model retains fourteen flowers, full reproductive organs and 840/336 cage
nodes. Updated seven-view specimen and overview images remain necessary for
visible acceptance of this local folded-wall correction.
