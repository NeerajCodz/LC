# Nasturtium — Tropaeolum majus

Reviewed 8 October 2026. [NC State](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/tropaeolum-majus/) describes spurred five-petaled flowers and alternate, long-petioled peltate leaves. [Info Flora's species account](https://www.infoflora.ch/en/flora/tropaeolum-majus.html) corroborates eight stamens, two larger upper petals, three lower petals with fringed claws and a three-lobed stigma. The [Smithsonian family account](https://www.naturalhistory.si.edu/sites/default/files/media/file/tropaeolaceae_0.pdf) also records the calyx spur and eight stamens. The peltate insertion remains inside the leaf disk.

Authored composition: three orange bilateral flowers plus a bud; five sepals with a hollow dorsal spur, two broad upper petals and three clawed/fringed lower petals, eight stamens and a three-branched style. Peltate blades meet their petioles within the disk; the herbaceous shoot is supported by its own rooted geometry.

Surface: orange/red guide veins, thin wrinkled petals and radial foliar veins. Lower petals and upper guide petals flex differently; spur insertion is fixed. Counts, geometry, pigments and mechanics are authored. Tendril-like petiole grasping, nectar delivery and measured pollination forces are outside scope.
