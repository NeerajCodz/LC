# Petunia — Petunia axillaris

Reviewed October 7, 2026. [Kew's Petunia account](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A331845-2/general-information)
specifically diagnoses P. axillaris by its white corolla and long narrow tube.
Genus-level morphology supplies five-lobed fused corollas, five often unequal
stamens, included stigma and glandular-pubescent leaves. [Kew's species record](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A191252-2/general-information)
provides name context.

Author three flowers and two buds on a leafy branched shoot, white tissue with
subtle green throat/veins, long narrow tubes and five calyx lobes. Tube walls
remain continuous through folding; included organs fit their closed shells.
Thin limbs flutter more than the anchored tube base and leaves.

Genus evidence does not establish species-specific hair density, exact stamen
ratios or measured optics; those details are authored. This is not a colored
garden-hybrid claim, and scent/night pollination are not modeled.
