# Canna — Canna indica

Research reviewed 12 October 2026. This dossier guides a dedicated authored 3D
study; inventory mapping and visual plausibility do not establish scientific validation.

## Source-backed traits

Asymmetric flowers have three sepals and three tube-connected true petals, two or three showy outer staminodes, a reflexed labellum, one fertile half-stamen, a petaloid style and an inferior ovary. Leaves are large, alternate and sheathing.

[Primary reference](https://www.iplant.cn/foc/pdf/Cannaceae.pdf).

## Authored implementation

Three flowers and one bud; narrow true petals beneath three orange-red staminodes, a recurved labellum, a flat style and a single anther attached to its fertile filament.

Foliage: Broad ovate-oblong leaves, sheathing bases and pinnate/parallel ribs.

Behavior: Distinct responses for supple staminodes, substantial leaves and the heavy shoot. Bloom can be reversed for inspection; this is not a
claim of biological reopening. Contact, stiffness, damping, colors, counts of
flowers/leaves, dimensions and stage timing are authored. Soft tissue uses sealed
parametric shells with separate folded positions/normals; fine reproductive organs
retain real geometry. Shared atlas sampling supplies cell/pigment irregularity,
with restrained scatter and distinct tissue roughness. Focused/selected physics
uses the existing 2,048/512-node budget; distant views remain ambient.

## Evidence gaps

C. indica has narrower floral parts than many garden Canna hybrids; no generic six-petal lily form is substituted. Proportions and authored composition counts are not measurements. No measured elastic constants, spectral pigment data or tissue scans are
available. Full seasonal growth, pollinator action and fertilization are outside
this study. Review front, side, 45 degrees, macro, closed bud and half bloom before
calling the specimen finished.
