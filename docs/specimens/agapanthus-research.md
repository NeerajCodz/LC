# Agapanthus — Agapanthus praecox

Research reviewed 12 October 2026. This dossier guides a dedicated authored 3D
study; inventory mapping and visual plausibility do not establish scientific validation.

## Source-backed traits

Rhizomatous plants carry strap-like leaves and umbels above the foliage. A. praecox supplies most cultivated evergreen agapanthus; cultivated plants are often mislabelled A. africanus.

[Primary reference](https://pza.sanbi.org/agapanthus-praecox)
[Additional reference](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/3/5/3503).

## Authored implementation

Eighteen outward-facing blue funnels and three buds with individual pedicels converging on one umbel insertion; six lobes and stamens per flower.

Foliage: Arching basal strap leaves and parallel veins.

Behavior: Pedicels nod independently while all remain anchored to the umbel; restrained fleshy-rim contact. Bloom can be reversed for inspection; this is not a
claim of biological reopening. Contact, stiffness, damping, colors, counts of
flowers/leaves, dimensions and stage timing are authored. Soft tissue uses sealed
parametric shells with separate folded positions/normals; fine reproductive organs
retain real geometry. Shared atlas sampling supplies cell/pigment irregularity,
with restrained scatter and distinct tissue roughness. Focused/selected physics
uses the existing 2,048/512-node budget; distant views remain ambient.

## Evidence gaps

The documented study is A. praecox, with sub-specific/cultivar identity unspecified. Flower count and directions are authored; SANBI full-page access was unavailable, supplemented by NParks. No measured elastic constants, spectral pigment data or tissue scans are
available. Full seasonal growth, pollinator action and fertilization are outside
this study. Review front, side, 45 degrees, macro, closed bud and half bloom before
calling the specimen finished.
