# Amaryllis — Hippeastrum puniceum

Research reviewed 12 October 2026. This dossier guides a dedicated authored 3D
study; inventory mapping and visual plausibility do not establish scientific validation.

## Source-backed traits

Hippeastrum has a stout leafless scape, two enclosing spathe bracts, trumpets of six basally fused tepals, six stamens, a trifid stigma and smooth strap foliage.

[Primary reference](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/hippeastrum/)
[Additional reference](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:122815-2).

## Authored implementation

Two orange-red flowers and one younger bud, a short continuous basal tube, unequal broad tepals, arched filaments and a three-branched stigma.

Foliage: Long, thick strap blades in a basal fan.

Behavior: Heavy funnels nod slowly; tepal rims yield independently and recover elastically. Bloom can be reversed for inspection; this is not a
claim of biological reopening. Contact, stiffness, damping, colors, counts of
flowers/leaves, dimensions and stage timing are authored. Soft tissue uses sealed
parametric shells with separate folded positions/normals; fine reproductive organs
retain real geometry. Shared atlas sampling supplies cell/pigment irregularity,
with restrained scatter and distinct tissue roughness. Focused/selected physics
uses the existing 2,048/512-node budget; distant views remain ambient.

## Evidence gaps

Common-name Amaryllis here denotes Hippeastrum, not Amaryllis belladonna. Kew accepts H. puniceum; genus-level organ traits inform an authored species study, with no cultivar identity claimed. No measured elastic constants, spectral pigment data or tissue scans are
available. Full seasonal growth, pollinator action and fertilization are outside
this study. Review front, side, 45 degrees, macro, closed bud and half bloom before
calling the specimen finished.

## Additional primary morphology and construction

[Flora of Bhutan, H. J. Noltie / Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh (1994),
as reproduced by the Bhutan Biodiversity Portal](https://biodiversity.bt/species/show/6613)
was reviewed on 12 October 2026. It describes horizontal orange-red trumpets with
a pale green/yellow inner base, a relatively short tube, narrowly ovate pointed
lobes, shorter filaments and a style extending a little beyond the stamens. Its
genus description supports declinate filaments that curve up at their ends and
a three-lobed stigma of variable depth. The model uses three short terminal
stigma lobes rather than conspicuous long divergent forks. This is a source-led
clarification of the planned trifid tip, not a measured species reconstruction.

The dedicated `AMARYLLIS_MODEL` retains two open flowers and one narrow younger
bud. Every flower has six curved, unequal overlapping tepals, a continuous short
basal tube, six minute throat scales, six arched filaments, two chambers per
transverse dorsifixed anther, an inferior ovary and the curved style. Exactly two
sealed scarious spathes belong to the umbel. All three pedicels converge at one
insertion and end at the lower ovary insertions. Six long, glossy, thick leaves
form a basal fan; this composition shows developed leaves together with flowers
for inspection, while the source describes leaves developing fully afterwards.

The focused model measures 910 desktop and 372 constrained cage nodes. Unit
checks cover exact counts, horizontal orientations, attached converging
pedicels, arched stamens, unequal tepal widths, tube/tepal continuity, enclosed
bud tips, foliage thickness and all sealed-surface/morph-normal checks.
Color, thickness, dimensions, display timing and mechanical coefficients remain
authored. Visual seven-view and browser inspection follow catalog integration.

The younger bud keeps its six original tepals fully imbricated. At their pointed
tips the blades overlap across one shared apex while retaining finite width and
physical thickness. Folded geometry uses the same closure; open flowers retain
their unequal tepal lengths. This closes the bud with actual tepal surfaces,
without auxiliary opaque shapes or collapsed apex vertices.
