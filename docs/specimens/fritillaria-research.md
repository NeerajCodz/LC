# Crown Imperial — Fritillaria imperialis

Sources reviewed October 13, 2026. Depict an orange-red study of
_Fritillaria imperialis_ L. The pinned inventory candidate is
`wfo-0000767851`; no named cultivar or botanical variety is claimed.

[Flora of Pakistan's species treatment](https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=5&taxon_id=250095930)
documents a robust shoot, sessile foliage in 3–4 whorls, an exposed peduncle,
and (1)–3–5 orange/red, broadly bell-shaped flowers on nodding pedicels.
Leaflike bracts form a tuft above the false umbel. Tepals are approximately
4–5.5(–7) cm long; the nearly circular white nectaries are 4–5(–8) mm across.
Long filaments bear elongated anthers; a three-grooved, exserted style ends in
a tripartite stigma. These dimensions are botanical reference ranges.
[The genus treatment](https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=5&taxon_id=113029)
provides six distinct tepals in two whorls, six epitepalous stamens and basal
tepal nectaries. Its key distinguishes the leafy crown and orange-red
_F. imperialis_ from another regional bright-yellow species.
[NC State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/fritillaria-imperialis/)
confirms drooping bells, the leaflike bract crown, and glossy lanceolate leaves
with wavy margins around the lower part of the stem. Its discussion of
checkerboard markings refers to _F. meleagris_, not this study's pigment.

Authored composition: five hanging bells beneath twelve narrow crown
bracts and three lower foliage whorls, each with six leaves. Preserve the
vertical sequence of leafy lower shoot, exposed peduncle, hanging flower ring
and upward crown. Each bell uses six separately sealed, overlapping curved
tepals, six filament/anther units, a central ovary and style with three stigma
divisions, and one pale basal nectary on each tepal. The bell is an arrangement
of distinct tepals, not a fused tubular corolla. Crown bracts are narrower than
lower foliage. Scene counts, palette, tepal thickness and proportions are
authored choices, including the reference-range choices for bells and leaves.

The six epitepalous filament bases use matching samples of their actual sealed
tepals in both open and folded poses. The selected midrib landmark, u = 0.5 and
v = 1/32, is authored. Generated root-cap/tepal-wall distance checks at low and
high quality cover closed, half and full bloom; they verify geometric
attachment without claiming measured tissue insertion dimensions.

Each pale nectary remains a sealed pad on the inner basal tepal face, rather
than an external oval. The authored pad centerline uses the u = 0.5, v = 0.1
tepal landmark in both bloom poses, with a 0.009 model-unit inward radial
setback. Its broad cross-section follows the circumferential tepal tangent;
the thin axis faces the wall. This corrects the previous global ellipse
orientation, which exposed two pads through the outer tepal. The setback,
flattening and section roll are construction choices, not measured nectary
microgeometry or a nectar-flow model.

Actual generated-buffer checks cover all six pads in all five bells, every
geometry tier, and closed/half/full bloom. Exterior center and upper/lower
silhouette rays must encounter tepal tissue first; interior rays must reach
the nectary before the wall. Pad exits overlap the inner wall, actual root-cap
centers stay inside the tepal solid, and both open/folded normals stay finite
and unit length. Counts, body cages, indexed inputs and draw partitions remain
unchanged. Updated interior and seven-view rendered acceptance is pending.

Flower pedicels retain their nodding rest orientation during authored bud
expansion and may yield locally while the shoot follows its anchored bend.
The leafy crown must remain attached above their insertion points. Never
rotate the entire plant around its flower ring. Opening trajectory, phase,
contact response, stiffness, damping, nectar-drop geometry and flow are not
measured by these sources. Reversible bloom is an inspection convention;
pollinators, nectar secretion, fruit development and calibrated optics are
outside the simulation.
