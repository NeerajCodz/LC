# Treasure flower · Gazania rigens

Reviewed 29 October 2026. This is a cultivated, eye-marked _G. rigens_ study, closest to var. _rigens_; it does not represent every variety or horticultural hybrid.

## Botanical evidence

[NSW Flora Online, species treatment](https://plantnet.rbgsyd.nsw.gov.au/cgi-bin/NSWfl.pl?lvl=sp&name=Gazania~rigens&page=nswfl) describes a trailing, leafy perennial, clustered narrow or divided leaves, green upper surfaces and white woolly lower surfaces. Solitary heads have orange/yellow rays marked with a black base and a white spot. Its [genus treatment](https://plantnet.rbgsyd.nsw.gov.au/cgi-bin/NSWfl.pl?lvl=gn&name=Gazania&page=nswfl) distinguishes one row of sterile rays from five-parted bisexual tubular disk flowers.

[SANBI's treatment of var. leucolaena](https://pza.sanbi.org/gazania-rigens-var-leucolaena) explicitly compares the varieties: eye-marked var. _rigens_ has green upper leaf surfaces, whereas white-woolly var. _leucolaena_ lacks the basal eye. The latter's description documents a partly joined involucral cup and free pointed phyllary tips. Its whole-plant whiteness is therefore not applied to this specimen. SANBI's search-index text was accessible; its direct page returned HTTP 403 during this review.

[Kew's species treatment, incorporating Flora Zambesiaca](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A208720-1/general-information) independently documents the lower two-thirds of the involucre being fused, variable entire/divided foliage, rolled margins and a felted underside except along the midrib. Those traits guide the dedicated geometry.

## Authored construction

Twenty curved, sealed sterile ray limbs surround 96 instanced true tubular disk flowers. The disk prototype has a continuous five-lobed corolla rim, five filaments, ten anther chambers, one style shaft and two style arms. Ray basal tubes connect the limbs to the head; no ray reproductive organs are invented. One continuous sealed cup and 24 pointed free apices replace separate generic sepals. Counts, golden-angle placement, size, tilt and the orange longitudinal ray stripe are artistic choices, not species constants or a named cultivar identification. The black basal marking and white eye follow the source-backed cultivated form.

Seven basal-clustered leaves have continuous connected pinnatifid blades, shallow midrib relief and rolled-back edges. The material distinguishes green tops, pale felted backs and different roughness. Fine wool is a material approximation rather than individually modeled fibers. The upright peduncle and leaf insertions use BotanicalStem's anchored clamped curve; the short trailing vegetative shoot is compressed into the basal cluster.

The head carrier rotates about its insertion. Macro framing uses its transformed center. Ray surfaces have matched folded positions/normals and separate contact cages. Whole-plant head cages contain 530 desktop or 264 constrained-device nodes, below the shared 2048/512 ceilings. All disk instances remain present at every tier; tessellation changes with quality. Shared rendering owns disposal and initializes morph targets.

## Validation and limits

The dedicated tests exercise generated sealed shells, positive thickness, deterministic buffers, unit open/folded normals, nondegenerate bud/half/full triangles, rooted insertion, five-lobed disk anatomy, sterile rays, lobed foliage and both contact ceilings. The reversible bloom pose is an authored visual transition, not a measured account of light-dependent opening. Dimensions, pigments, material response and mechanical constants are uncalibrated. Browser view review, whole-garden costs and device evidence belong to the batch validation record; geometry tests alone do not establish photographic equivalence or frame rate.
