# Rosemary Grevillea — Grevillea rosmarinifolia

Reviewed 2 November 2026. Taxon: *Grevillea rosmarinifolia*, Proteaceae. [Implementation guide](../specimens/grevillea.md).

## Evidence and reference proportions

[Australian Plants Society](https://resources.austplants.com.au/plant/grevillea-rosmarinifolia/) identifies this species as a compact **spider-flower**, with paired flowers, four tepals, four stamens, red carpels and red perianths sometimes tinged cream at their tips. It is not one of the genus’s one-sided toothbrush forms. Leaves are alternate, narrow and rolled under, with silky gray hairs below. [NSW Flora / PlantNET](https://plantnet.rbgsyd.nsw.gov.au/cgi-bin/NSWfl.pl?lvl=sp&name=Grevillea~rosmarinifolia&page=nswfl) supports the revolute margins, pink/red perianth with a possible cream apex, and lateral pollen presenter; its gynoecium is 15–22.5 mm long.

## Authored study

Six pairs provide twelve flowers on a short branched display shoot. Each has four individually curling perianth sections, four attached sessile anthers, a long curved red style, flattened lateral cream presenter, stigma and ovary. The cream distal pigment belongs to the original perianth walls; there are no overlapping cream patch shells. Narrow distal ribbons curl apart at full bloom instead of completing a bowl.

Eighteen alternate leaves on a 1.36-unit shoot have real revolute margins, green upper faces and gray lower pigment. Counts, dimensions, degree of curl and vein relief are authored. The petal material uses roughness 0.54 and scattering 0.045. Fine hairs are suggested through relief and tissue response, not a full modeled hair coat.

Opening releases the curled style/perianth arrangement. Distinct flower nods and petal contact use 1,440 / 432 nodes; the woody planting point stays anchored and leaf tips yield above their insertions.
