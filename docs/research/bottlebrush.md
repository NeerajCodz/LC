# Crimson Bottlebrush — Melaleuca citrina

Research reviewed 12 October 2026. This dossier guides a dedicated authored 3D
study; inventory mapping and visual plausibility do not establish scientific validation.

## Source-backed traits

A woody shrub has narrow stiff leaves with visible veins. Cylindrical spikes have bright red filaments and dark anthers; the brush consists of stamens rather than petals.

[Primary reference](https://plantnet.rbgsyd.nsw.gov.au/cgi-bin/NSWfl.pl?page=nswfl&lvl=sp&name=Callistemon~citrinus)
[Additional reference](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/2/7/2772).

## Authored implementation

Twenty-four tiny true flowers distributed around a cylindrical shoot, each with five small petals and twenty-four red filaments bearing paired anther chambers. Sealed prototypes share draw resources.

Foliage: Alternate narrow pointed leaves, subdued oil-gland pattern and woody shoot.

Behavior: Independent true-flower motion with slender-filament bend; detailed cages protect the hypanthial attachments without simulating every fine anther. Bloom can be reversed for inspection; this is not a
claim of biological reopening. Contact, stiffness, damping, colors, counts of
flowers/leaves, dimensions and stage timing are authored. Soft tissue uses sealed
parametric shells with separate folded positions/normals; fine reproductive organs
retain real geometry. Shared atlas sampling supplies cell/pigment irregularity,
with restrained scatter and distinct tissue roughness. Focused/selected physics
uses the existing 2,048/512-node budget; distant views remain ambient.

## Evidence gaps

The source uses Callistemon citrinus; NParks recognizes the Melaleuca combination. Floret/stamen counts and optical/mechanical constants are authored rather than species invariants. No measured elastic constants, spectral pigment data or tissue scans are
available. Full seasonal growth, pollinator action and fertilization are outside
this study. Review front, side, 45 degrees, macro, closed bud and half bloom before
calling the specimen finished.
