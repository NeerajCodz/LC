Project LC aims to bring every single flower in the world to life in 3D. These notes cover the specimens developed so far and expand with the collection; they do not define the limits of the project.

The source-backed global target list is [FLOWERS.md](FLOWERS.md), derived from the [World Flora Online June 2026 release](https://zenodo.org/records/20782718), licensed CC0. Its names and ancestry provide a taxonomic inventory, not morphology or measured mechanical parameters. Existing study mappings are exact-name candidates for review; ornamental Rose and Lily concepts currently require taxonomic clarification. The [blue passionflower dossier](research/passionflower.md) documents the supported vine specimen, organ construction and remaining evidence gaps.

Botanical sources reviewed September 9, 2026; implementation notes updated September 10, 2026. The collection uses authored procedural geometry rather than photographic textures or scanned plants. The references below guide organ arrangement, surface character, and foliage. Cultivar variation is substantial: the Rose, Peony, Dahlia, and Chrysanthemum represent ornamental double forms, not every form of their species.

## Additional floral forms

The expanded collection includes Poppy, Daffodil, Bearded Iris, Calla Lily, Anthurium, Columbine, Bleeding Heart, and Bird of Paradise. See the [source-linked specimen dossier](architecture/expanded-forms.md) for anatomy, construction, opening behavior, and explicit modeling limitations.

Common Morning Glory adds a single fused funnel with twisted bud geometry, included stamens, cordate leaves and a hairy twining shoot. Its [dossier](research/morning-glory.md) distinguishes the reversible inspection animation from the different biological processes of opening and mature closure.

## Lotus center revision

The yellow structures around the receptacle are stamens, which carry pollen. A flower at anthesis should not have the conspicuous mature seed pattern of a dry seed head. The model now has shallow carpel sockets and small stigma tips on a continuous, tapered receptacle, surrounded by 156 individually posed stamens with paired anther chambers and pale connective appendages. The count and dimensions are artistic choices, not a species-wide anatomical constant.

The lotus leaf is a curved, sealed, circular blade with radial veins and a separate petiole rising from the plant base, independent of the flower stalk.

## Rendering and interpretation

The October 2026 additions use dedicated studies of [Gazania](research/gazania.md), [Scarlet Sage](research/salvia.md), [Flowering Tobacco](research/nicotiana.md), [Lantana](research/lantana.md) and [Purple Coneflower](research/echinacea.md). They distinguish true disk florets from showy sterile rays, bilateral and salverform tubes, persistent calyces, woody cluster supports and species foliage. Each dossier separates botanical evidence from authored counts, pigments, opening trajectories and mechanical response.

Macro views keep high pixel density and use finer petal subdivisions. Depth-of-field and glow effects are omitted to preserve visible organ detail. Pigments, subtle papillae, vein relief, and back-light response remain real-time material approximations. Flowering is a reversible artistic animation, not a time-resolved biological simulation.

WebGL 2 renders these structures and their physical materials. vgpu supplies procedural tissue data and studio HDR illumination when WebGPU is available; matching GLSL/CPU fallbacks preserve viewing on WebGL-only devices. These generated textures encode surface properties and light, not images of flowers.

The header/favicon flower emblem and the blooming SVG loading ornament are interface graphics. The loader intentionally depicts a stylized flower rather than a particular species; it is separate from the modeled specimens and is not a botanical reference.

The collection is a botanical art experience. It is not a scientific identification key, a measured anatomical reconstruction, or a claim of photographic equivalence.

Hardy Fuchsia uses narrow spreading sepals and short purple petals of _Fuchsia magellanica_, rather than the broad, reflexed or ruffled forms of ornamental hybrids. Its [research dossier](research/fuchsia.md) records authoritative floras, organ counts, proportions and the limits of its authored hanging-flower mechanics.

The next five studies preserve different constructions: [Carnation](research/carnation.md) has a cultivated double, fringed head and jointed opposite-leaved shoot; [Plumeria](research/plumeria.md) has waxy overlapping corolla limbs and succulent branches; [Foxglove](research/foxglove.md) has a one-sided raceme of hollow, spotted bells; [Sweet Pea](research/sweet-pea.md) has bilateral banners, wings and enclosing keels on a supported winged shoot; [Bougainvillea](research/bougainvillea.md) distinguishes papery bracts from its true five-angled floral tubes. Their dossiers link primary botanical and mechanics sources and identify authored proportions, counts, colors and unmeasured parameters.

[Cyclamen](research/cyclamen.md) adds reflexed, twisted lobes on separate basal stalks and silver foliage; [Snapdragon](research/snapdragon.md) adds a bilateral closed throat and reversible lower-palate pressure; [Hardy Begonia](research/hardy-begonia.md) separates four-tepalled male and three-tepalled female flowers, unequal ovary wings and asymmetric leaves. [Lacecap Hydrangea](research/hydrangea.md) separates showy marginal sepals from fertile central florets; [King Protea](research/king-protea.md) separates overlapping bracts from true perianths, anthers and pollen-presenting styles. The dossiers record historical-name/genus-level evidence and species-specific evidence gaps explicitly.
