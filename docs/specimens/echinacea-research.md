# Eastern purple coneflower · Echinacea purpurea

Reviewed 29 October 2026. This is a single-flowered pink-purple _E. purpurea_ study, without an asserted cultivar identity.

## Botanical evidence

[Flora of North America, species treatment](https://floranorthamerica.org/Echinacea_purpurea) records fibrous roots, usually hairy herbage, ovate to narrow leaves with three or five principal veins and commonly toothed margins. Its rays spread or curve back; the disk is conic. Sharp, red-orange palea tips are separate from the greenish/pink/purple disk corolla lobes. These traits distinguish the specimen from narrow-leaved pale coneflowers and flat-disk daisies.

[FNA's genus treatment](https://floranorthamerica.org/Echinacea) identifies basal and alternate stem leaves, smaller distal blades, solitary heads, several phyllary rows, sterile rays and bisexual five-lobed disk florets. Receptacular paleae exceed the corollas, have keeled bodies and abrupt awnlike tips; they create the characteristic spiny cone. The flora gives 200–300 or more disk florets, so this model's reduced 112-floret arrangement is explicitly an authored rendering approximation, not a botanical count. The genus treatment also supports the 21-ray upper end used here.

## Authored construction

Twenty-one separate sealed ray limbs extend from narrow basal tubes, form longitudinal relief and curve downward. Their thickness and smoothly cupped cross-sections remain present at macro scale. A continuous tapered receptacle carries 112 true disk-floret instances. Each prototype contains a five-lobed tubular corolla, five stamens with paired anther chambers, a style shaft and two style arms. Each also has a pointed, flattened rusty-orange palea, taller than the corolla. Its sealed tapered organ approximates the keeled palea body and awn; detailed scarious margins and ovary walls are not reconstructed. Twenty-four lanceolate phyllaries occupy two series beneath the rays, rather than forming a second petal whorl.

Five alternately inserted leaves become smaller up the stalk. Their real petiole regions lead to broad ovate, serrated blades with three raised primary-vein tracks and fine relief. Secondary venation and rough pubescence use the shared leaf material. Ten to 72 short sealed hair shells per blade, depending on quality, provide actual small silhouette detail; they are not a measured hair density or cellular reconstruction. The complete blade/hair assembly keeps matching folded positions and normals.

Cone heights, ray lengths, pigment shades, phyllotaxis, leaf placement and deformation constants are authored. The head carrier rotates around its insertion while BotanicalStem preserves anchored leaf and peduncle motion. Macro focus uses the transformed cone center. Ray contact surfaces and the receptacle obstacle use 554 desktop or 276 constrained nodes, below the 2048/512 ceilings. Disk counts remain unchanged across quality tiers. The reversible bud-to-drooping-ray transition is not growth, senescence or reopening biology.

## Validation and limits

Dedicated tests evaluate generated closed topology, thickness, deterministic surfaces, unit open/folded normals and nondegenerate triangles at bud/half/full bloom. Additional cases check ray droop, sterile versus fertile anatomy, relative palea height, actual conic floret placements, rooted insertion, petiole/blade proportions, sealed hair assemblies and quality-dependent detail. Source-backed anatomy does not calibrate pigment optics or mechanical response. The batch record separately owns seven-view browser inspection, renderer costs and device limits; these geometry checks do not claim photorealism or a frame-rate measurement.
