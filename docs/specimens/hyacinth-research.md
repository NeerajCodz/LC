# Hyacinth — Hyacinthus orientalis

Research reviewed 12 October 2026. This dossier guides a dedicated authored 3D
study; inventory mapping and visual plausibility do not establish scientific validation.

## Source-backed traits

Many waxy tubular flowers form a cylindrical scapose raceme. Each has six spreading/reflexed lobes; four to six fleshy basal leaves have upturned margins.

[Primary reference](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/hyacinthus-orientalis/).

## Authored implementation

Eighteen blossoms and three smaller buds distributed around one stout scape; continuous six-lobed tubes, six included stamens and a style.

Foliage: Five thick, keeled strap leaves with parallel veins.

Behavior: Fleshy limbs have lower compliance than bluebell; opening progresses along the authored display. Bloom can be reversed for inspection; this is not a
claim of biological reopening. Contact, stiffness, damping, colors, counts of
flowers/leaves, dimensions and stage timing are authored. Soft tissue uses sealed
parametric shells with separate folded positions/normals; fine reproductive organs
retain real geometry. Shared atlas sampling supplies cell/pigment irregularity,
with restrained scatter and distinct tissue roughness. Focused/selected physics
uses the existing 2,048/512-node budget; distant views remain ambient.

## Evidence gaps

A cultivated pink single form is selected. Counts, exact lobe curvature, stage delays and elastic coefficients are authored; source family terminology is older than the catalog classification. No measured elastic constants, spectral pigment data or tissue scans are
available. Full seasonal growth, pollinator action and fertilization are outside
this study. Review front, side, 45 degrees, macro, closed bud and half bloom before
calling the specimen finished.
